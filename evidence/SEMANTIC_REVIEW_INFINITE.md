Reverse paraphrase for Hilbert's Hotel: a finite full hotel cannot free a room merely by moving guests injectively among its rooms, while an infinite numbered hotel can move the guest in room n to room n+1 and free room 1. This motivates the definition that a set is Dedekind-infinite exactly when it injects into a proper subset, equivalently when it has an injective self-map whose range omits some member.

Reverse paraphrase for Dedekind algebras: the intended natural-number surrogate has a distinguished zero outside the successor range, an injective successor, and no elements beyond those generated from zero by repeated successor application. The closure of a seed under a self-map is the intersection of all closed sets containing it; it contains the seed, is closed and lies inside every other such set. Restricting the injection witnessing a Dedekind-infinite set to the closure of an omitted element produces a Dedekind algebra. BN-SRC-018 makes the closure lemma's ambient carrier explicit: the frozen statement uses o in A without binding A, and its proof that range(f) union {o} is closed needs f to be a self-map of that carrier.

Reverse paraphrase for induction: if a subset-like predicate holds of the distinguished zero and is preserved by successor for carrier elements that satisfy it, then it holds throughout the Dedekind algebra. The formula schema permits additional parameters by universally closing their free variables. Addition, multiplication and exponentiation are later fixed recursively by the displayed base and successor clauses. BN-SRC-017 repairs the frozen proof's direct appeal to the closure minimality against X: X need not be globally successor-closed. The intersection of N and X contains the zero and is successor-closed because N is closed and the stated premise supplies membership in X; minimality therefore yields N contained in X from exactly the theorem's hypotheses.

Reverse paraphrase for Dedekind's philosophical argument: any Dedekind-infinite set supplies a structure with the required natural-number behavior, and all such structures are isomorphic, but this does not identify one carrier as the natural numbers themselves. The first quoted passage presents arithmetic, algebra and analysis as products of pure laws of thought. The second argues from the indefinitely iterable thought that a thought can itself be thought. The section then states the technical gap rather than endorsing the argument: infinitely many possible thoughts have not thereby been shown to form one set. The move beyond informal set selection is left as the source's concluding motivation.

Reverse paraphrase for Schröder–Bernstein: closure generalizes from one seed to a seed set. If A is contained in B, B in C, and A is equinumerous with C, a bijection from C to A and the closure of C minus B define a map that uses the bijection on the closure and the identity elsewhere; it is a bijection from C to B. Applying this helper to g(f(A)) contained in g(B) contained in A, followed by the inverse of g on its image, turns injections both ways into a bijection A to B. The source's nested cardinal-equality macro is retained; it expands to the chained statement A approximately B approximately C. The range proof spells out the nontrivial inclusion from B into the range, while the reverse inclusion follows immediately from the two cases defining the map.
