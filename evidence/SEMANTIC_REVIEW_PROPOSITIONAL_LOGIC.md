Reverse paraphrase for the introductory scope: propositional formulas are built from variables by truth-functional connectives. Once a valuation gives each occurring variable one of the two truth values, the connective truth functions determine the whole formula's value. The account deliberately leaves aside necessity, knowledge, tense and non-bivalent possibilities. It also distinguishes the truth-functional material conditional from disputed ordinary-language conditionals. Formation rules define formulas inductively and, when properly designed, give each formula one structural reading; semantic evaluation and satisfaction can then be defined by induction on that structure. Tautology, entailment and satisfiability are derived by quantifying over valuations.

Reverse paraphrase for formulas: the language contains a denumerable stock of propositional variables, whichever constants and connectives the selected tags make primitive, and punctuation. Other displayed operators may be defined abbreviations, which shorten formulas and reduce the number of primitive proof cases. The inductive definition first admits atomic formulas, then closes under each active connective, and excludes everything else. The alternative-symbol discussion preserves the distinctions among negation, conjunction, conditional, biconditional, falsity and truth. Reverse paraphrase for syntactic structure and preliminary results: syntactic identity means equal-length symbol strings with the same symbol at every position, including strings described by concatenation. Formula induction follows because the formulas are the least class containing the atoms and closed under the constructors. Balanced parentheses, absence of formula-valued proper initial segments and unique parsing then follow. The unique-readability proof's representative conditional case first forces identical left subformulas and then invokes the induction hypothesis on the right subformula. Reverse paraphrase for formation sequences: a finite bottom-up sequence witnesses a formula when its last entry is that formula and every entry is atomic or is made from earlier entries by an active connective. Entries may repeat or be unused. Formula induction gives a formation sequence for every inductively generated formula. Conversely, strong induction on the last index shows that every string with such a sequence belongs to the inductively defined formula set, using proper initial subsequences for the immediate components. The Bengali prose calls the induction measure the final index, avoiding ambiguity between the index \(n\) and the \(n+1\) entries of a zero-indexed sequence. BN-SRC-020 repairs the conjunction case where the frozen proof switches from the final entry \(A_n\) to \(A\) and from syntactic identity to an equivalence symbol; the repaired statement matches both the governing definition and the case list immediately above it.

Reverse paraphrase for valuations and satisfaction: a valuation maps propositional variables to True or False. Its recursively extended evaluation function handles constants, negation, conjunction, disjunction, material conditional and biconditional exactly as the preserved truth tables specify. The ternary-connective exercise returns the second argument's value when the first is true and the third argument's value when it is false. Local determination says two valuations that agree on the variables occurring in the fixed formula give that formula the same value. Satisfaction is then defined constructor by constructor and is proved equivalent to evaluation as True; satisfaction of a set requires satisfaction of every member under the same valuation.

Reverse paraphrase for the semantic notions: a formula is satisfiable when at least one valuation satisfies it, unsatisfiable when none does, tautological when all do, and contingent when satisfiable but not tautological. A premise set entails a formula exactly when every valuation satisfying the whole premise set also satisfies the formula. The listed consequences preserve empty-premise tautology, semantic modus ponens, finite-subset preservation, monotonicity and transitivity. Entailment of \(A\) is equivalent to unsatisfiability after adjoining its negation, and the semantic deduction theorem moves one premise \(A\) into the material conditional \(A \lif B\). The exercises remain requests for proofs rather than completed arguments.
