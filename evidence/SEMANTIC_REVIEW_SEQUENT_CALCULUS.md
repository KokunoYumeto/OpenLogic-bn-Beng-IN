Reverse paraphrase of the proof-theoretic notions: a theorem is a sentence derivable with an empty antecedent. A sentence is derivable from a possibly infinite premise set when a finite subset, arranged as a finite antecedent sequence, yields it. Contraction, weakening and exchange make multiplicity and order immaterial for this finite witness. Inconsistency means that some finite subset derives an empty succedent. The resulting relation is reflexive, monotone and transitive by initial sequents, finite-subset inclusion and cut. Its compactness clauses say that derivability and inconsistency always have finite witnesses.
