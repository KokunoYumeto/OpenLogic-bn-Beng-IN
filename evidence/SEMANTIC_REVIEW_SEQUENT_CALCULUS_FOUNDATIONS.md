Reverse paraphrase for sequents and rules: a sequent has a finite, possibly empty antecedent and a finite, possibly empty succedent. It holds when truth of every antecedent sentence guarantees truth of at least one succedent sentence. Thus its associated sentence is the implication from the conjunction on the left to the disjunction on the right; an empty antecedent leaves only the right disjunction, and an empty succedent negates the left conjunction. Appending on either end and concatenating two finite sequences are distinguished. Initial sequents have A on both sides, with optional truth and falsity forms controlled by tags. Sequent derivations are trees rooted upward in initial sequents and extended downward by logical or structural rules.

Reverse paraphrase for the propositional rules: left and right negation move A across the sequent sign while negating it. The conjunction rules on the left admit either displayed conjunct form, while the right rule needs one premise for each conjunct. The disjunction rule on the left needs one premise for each disjunct, while either displayed right rule introduces the disjunction from one disjunct. Left conditional combines a derivation of its antecedent on the right with a derivation using its consequent on the left; right conditional moves the antecedent to the left and the consequent to the right. All surrounding sequence variables and premise/conclusion positions are preserved.

Reverse paraphrase for the quantifier rules: left universal and right existential instantiate with a closed term t. Right universal and left existential use a constant a absent from the lower sequent; this historically named eigenvariable must be fresh outside A(a) in the upper sequent. Writing A(t) abbreviates substitution for the displayed free variable. A term already present elsewhere in A may remain there when selected occurrences in the premise are abstracted into a bound variable. The later statement that t has no restrictions is read together with the earlier rule specification: there is no further eigenvariable-style freshness restriction beyond closedness. Without the eigenvariable condition, the two preserved trees would incorrectly derive existential A implies universal A.

Reverse paraphrase for structural rules: weakening adds a sentence on either side, contraction merges two adjacent identical sentences into one, and exchange swaps two adjacent sentences within a side. Repeated structural steps may be abbreviated by double inference lines. Cut combines a derivation ending with A on the right and one using A on the left, removes that linking sentence, and concatenates the remaining sequences. The source notes that cut is dispensable in principle but useful for reusing and combining derivations.

Reverse paraphrase for LK derivations: a derivation is a finite sequent tree whose topmost nodes are initial sequents, whose bottommost node is the specified end-sequent S, and whose every other node is a premise in a correct rule application whose conclusion sits directly below it. The worked example begins from two identity sequents, uses left weakening and exchange so both premises have the same left sequence, and then applies right conjunction. Reversing the two premises reverses the order of the two conjuncts. Every sequence assignment and every rule label in both final proof trees is preserved.
